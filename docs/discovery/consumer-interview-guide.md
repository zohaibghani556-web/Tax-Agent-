# Consumer Interview Guide

Purpose: validate the secondary consumer workflow and whether consumer intake can feed the same Tax File Graph used by firms. The consumer product should not become a consumer-only chatbot or filing replacement before the firm wedge is validated.

**Priority: secondary.** CPA/firm interviews (see `cpa-interview-guide.md`) must be scheduled and started first. Consumer interviews inform product design but do not determine the go/no-go decision for the firm wedge. Do not run consumer interviews as a substitute for the harder-to-schedule CPA conversations.

## Interview Setup

Target participants:

- Students.
- Young workers.
- First-time filers.
- Newcomers.
- DIY filers.
- Consumers who use a CPA or tax preparer.
- Consumers who start with DIY software but are unsure whether they did it correctly.

Opening script:

> We are researching how Canadians collect documents and prepare for tax filing. This is not tax advice and not a filing service. We want to understand confusion, document organization, trust, privacy, and whether a CPA-ready package would be useful.

Ground rules:

- Do not ask for SIN, exact income, client names, or private tax documents.
- Ask for examples using categories and ranges.
- Do not give tax advice during the interview.

## Profile

1. How did you file your most recent tax return?
2. Did you use DIY software, a CPA, a tax preparer, a family member, or paper forms?
3. How confident did you feel before filing?
4. What tax documents did you need to collect?
5. Were you a student, employee, self-employed, investor, renter, homeowner, newcomer, or someone with multiple situations?

## Student And Young Worker Confusion

1. What confused you most the first time you filed?
2. Did you understand which slips mattered?
3. Did you understand tuition slips, T4s, T5s, RRSP/FHSA receipts, rent, moving expenses, or student loan interest?
4. Did you know where to find documents from school, employers, banks, or CRA?
5. Did you worry about missing a document?
6. Did you know what to do if a document arrived after filing?
7. What would have made you feel more confident?

## DIY Filing Pain

1. Which tax software did you use?
2. What parts were easy?
3. What parts were confusing or stressful?
4. Did you understand why the software asked certain questions?
5. Did you ever leave a question blank because you were unsure?
6. Did you compare multiple tools or ask someone for help?
7. Did you know whether your final result was correct?
8. What did you do when the software explanation was unclear?

## Document Upload Willingness

1. Would you upload tax documents to a tool if it organized them for you?
2. Which documents would you be comfortable uploading?
3. Which documents would you not upload?
4. Would you upload photos from your phone?
5. Would you connect email, CRA, or school portals if that existed later?
6. Would you prefer manual upload over connectors?
7. What would you need to see before uploading documents?

## Trust And Privacy Concerns

1. What tax or identity information feels most sensitive?
2. Would Canadian data residency matter to you?
3. Would you care if a third-party AI provider processed extracted document text?
4. What privacy language would make you comfortable?
5. What privacy language would make you stop?
6. Would you expect the product to avoid collecting SIN?
7. Would you want to delete your data after filing?
8. Would you trust AI more if every number linked back to a document?

## CPA-Ready Package

1. Have you ever sent documents to a CPA or tax preparer?
2. Was it clear what they needed from you?
3. Did they ask follow-up questions?
4. Would it help to generate a package that organizes your documents, extracted values, missing items, and questions?
5. Would you use that package even if you still filed with a CPA or DIY software?
6. What should the package include?
7. Would a source-linked summary make you more confident?
8. Would you share the package with a CPA if it saved them time?

## Willingness To Pay

1. Would you pay for document organization before filing?
2. Would you pay for a CPA-ready package?
3. Would you pay more if a CPA reviewed it?
4. Would you prefer one-time filing-season pricing or a subscription?
5. What price feels reasonable?
6. What price would make you use free DIY tools instead?
7. Would students or first-time filers pay, or would this need to be free/low-cost?

## AI Boundaries

1. What would you trust AI to do in your taxes?
2. What would you not trust AI to do?
3. Would you trust AI to explain a tax concept?
4. Would you trust AI to extract a number from a slip?
5. Would you trust AI to calculate your tax?
6. Would you want clear language that tax math comes from deterministic software, not AI?
7. Would you want a human-review option?

## Stop-Using Triggers

1. What would make you stop using this product immediately?
2. Would one wrong extracted value break trust?
3. Would unclear privacy terms break trust?
4. Would you stop if it could not file directly?
5. Would you stop if it asked for too much information?
6. Would you stop if it could not explain missing documents?

## Concept Test

Use after understanding the user's workflow:

> Imagine a tool that lets you upload tax documents, organizes them into a tax-year file, extracts values, shows what is missing, links numbers back to source documents, and creates either a DIY prep summary or a CPA-ready package. It does not file your return directly. Would that help?

Follow-up questions:

1. What part is most useful?
2. What part is not useful?
3. Would this replace anything you use today?
4. Would you still want CPA help?
5. Would this make you more or less comfortable with taxes?
