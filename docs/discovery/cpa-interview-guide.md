# CPA Interview Guide

Purpose: validate whether TaxAgent's first wedge should be an AI T1 Intake + Review Workbench for Canadian small tax firms, and identify the minimum workflow, evidence, export, and trust requirements before building more product.

Use this as a discovery interview, not a sales demo. Ask about real recent workflow, not opinions in the abstract.

## Interview Setup

Target participants:

- Small CPA firms, bookkeepers, and independent tax preparers serving Canadian T1 clients.
- Firms using TaxCycle, ProFile, Taxprep, Cantax, DT Max, QuickBooks, Excel, DoxCycle, Dext, TaxDome, Canopy, Karbon, Caseware, email, or portal workflows.
- Owners, reviewers, senior preparers, and seasonal staff managers.

Opening script:

> We are researching how Canadian tax firms collect, review, and prepare T1 client files. We are not asking you to switch tax software, and this is not a filing product. We are exploring whether an intake and review workbench that organizes source documents, extractions, corrections, missing items, and source-linked workpapers would save time before your existing prep software.

Ground rules:

- Do not ask for client names, SINs, or confidential files.
- Ask for workflow examples using anonymized descriptions.
- Ask for ranges if exact numbers are sensitive.
- Avoid pitching until the end.
- Record only with permission.

## Firm Profile

1. What types of clients do you prepare T1 returns for?
2. Roughly how many T1 returns do you prepare in a season?
3. What percentage are simple, moderate, and complex?
4. How many staff touch a typical T1 file before final review?
5. What does tax season capacity look like for your firm?
6. Where do bottlenecks show up first: client chase, data entry, review, workpapers, or filing?
7. Which return types are profitable, and which feel like low-margin work?
8. For a typical simple T1 file, roughly how many minutes does your team spend on each phase: collecting documents, chasing missing items, entering data, reviewing, and preparing workpapers?
9. How does that change for a moderate-complexity file?
10. Do you hire seasonal staff? If so, how long does it take to train them on your intake and document-handling process?

## Current Intake Workflow

1. Walk me through the last T1 file you completed, from first client contact to final review.
2. How do clients send documents today?
3. Which channels create the most cleanup: email, portal, paper, phone, CRA downloads, or mixed uploads?
4. Who organizes source documents?
5. How are documents named, sorted, and attached to the file?
6. What intake checklist or organizer do you use?
7. How do you know a file is complete enough to start?
8. What usually causes a file to stall?
9. What still lives in spreadsheets or staff memory?
10. What part of intake would you most like to stop doing manually?
11. What do you do in the first five minutes after a client drops off documents?
12. Have you tried any tool, process change, or workaround in the last two years to speed up T1 intake? What happened?

## Software Stack

1. Which tax preparation software do you use for T1s?
2. Do you use TaxCycle, ProFile, Taxprep, Cantax, DT Max, or something else?
3. Do you use DoxCycle, Dext, QuickBooks Online Accountant, TaxDome, Canopy, Karbon, Caseware, Excel, or another tool in the T1 workflow?
4. Which tools are non-negotiable?
5. Which tools are tolerated because switching would be painful?
6. Where do you duplicate data between systems?
7. What export or import formats do your current tools accept?
8. If a new tool sat before your tax software, what would it need to output?

## CRA Auto-fill Usage

1. Do you use CRA Auto-fill for Returns?
2. For which clients or return types do you use it?
3. Which slips do you trust from Auto-fill?
4. Which items still require client-provided documents even when Auto-fill is available?
5. How often do client documents conflict with Auto-fill data?
6. How do you handle new clients where authorization or prior-year access is not yet set up?
7. Has Auto-fill reduced manual slip entry enough that OCR for T4/T5/T3 slips is not a major pain?
8. Where does Auto-fill fail to solve your workflow?

## Non-CRA Document Pain

1. Which documents create the most manual work because CRA does not already have them?
2. How painful are receipts, rental statements, self-employment records, tuition documents, medical expenses, donations, moving expenses, childcare, support payments, and investment details?
3. Which documents arrive as poor-quality photos or PDFs?
4. Which documents have ambiguous fields or require judgment?
5. Which moderate-complexity files create enough pain to justify new software?
6. Are simple/student/young-worker files painful enough to pay for, or are they already fast?
7. Which return segment should we target first if we want measurable time savings?

## Client Chasing

1. What are the most common missing items?
2. How do you track missing items today?
3. How many follow-up touches does a typical T1 file require?
4. Who sends the follow-ups?
5. How much time is lost waiting for clients?
6. Which missing items are easiest for software to detect?
7. Which missing items require professional judgment?
8. Would you trust software to draft client follow-up questions if your staff reviews them first?
9. What would make automated follow-up unsafe or annoying?

## Review Bottlenecks

1. What does reviewer review mean in your firm?
2. Which fields or documents do reviewers always check?
3. How do reviewers find the source behind a number?
4. How long does review take for simple, moderate, and complex files?
5. What kinds of errors do reviewers catch most often?
6. Which errors are caused by client documents, OCR, staff entry, software imports, or unclear rules?
7. What would help reviewers trust that a file is ready?
8. What would make reviewers reject an AI-assisted intake package?

## Source Documents And Workpapers

1. What workpapers do you prepare for T1 files today?
2. What does a good T1 workpaper package include?
3. How are source documents linked to entered numbers?
4. Do you need field-level source links, document-level links, or both?
5. How do reviewer notes and sign-offs work?
6. How do you archive source documents and workpapers?
7. Would a source-linked workpaper PDF be useful?
8. Would a CSV or JSON export be useful, or do you need a human-readable package first?
9. Which workpaper format would be acceptable for a pilot?

## Export Preferences

1. If TaxAgent prepared a reviewed intake package, where should the data go next?
2. Would you prefer CSV, Excel, JSON, PDF workpaper, copy/paste summary, or direct integration?
3. Which fields must be included in a minimum viable export?
4. Would you accept manual download and upload for a pilot?
5. How much cleanup would make an export not worth using?
6. Which tax software integration would matter first?
7. Would you prefer TaxAgent to stay a standalone workbench or integrate into your existing tools?

## Willingness To Pay

1. If this saved 15 minutes per file, what would that be worth?
2. If this saved 30 minutes per file, what would that be worth?
3. Would you prefer per-file pricing, seasonal pricing, monthly pricing, or a pilot fee?
4. What price would be too cheap to trust?
5. What price would require partner approval?
6. Would you pay for this before tax season as setup, during tax season as usage, or after seeing measured results?
7. What proof would you need before paying?
8. Would you pay more for moderate-complexity files than simple files?

## Concerns About AI

1. What worries you about AI in tax preparation?
2. Would you trust AI for document extraction?
3. Would you trust AI for missing-item detection?
4. Would you trust AI to draft client questions?
5. What tasks must remain deterministic or reviewer-approved?
6. What would you need to see to trust the evidence trail?
7. How should the product make clear that AI does not calculate tax or cite tax rules from memory?
8. What privacy, residency, or client consent concerns would block a pilot?

## Stop-Using Triggers

1. What would make you stop using the product after one file?
2. What correction rate would make it slower than doing the work manually?
3. How many false missing-item alerts are acceptable?
4. What kind of source-linking failure would break trust?
5. What export failure would make the workflow unusable?
6. What support response time would you need during tax season?
7. What would make staff resist using it?
8. What would make reviewers refuse to sign off?

## Concept Test

Use only after understanding the current workflow:

> We are considering an AI T1 Intake + Review Workbench. It would not replace your tax software or file returns. It would organize client documents, extract fields, preserve source evidence, track corrections, flag missing items, and produce a source-linked package for review and export. Would that fit before or around your current workflow?

Follow-up questions:

1. What part sounds most valuable?
2. What part sounds unnecessary?
3. What is missing?
4. Which client segment should we test first?
5. Would you review a prototype?
6. Would you provide anonymized sample workflow examples?
7. Would you consider a privacy-safe pilot with no filing promises?
8. When would you realistically test a new intake tool: during tax season when the pain is real, or off-season when you have bandwidth?
9. Who in your firm would need to approve trying a new tool in the workflow?
